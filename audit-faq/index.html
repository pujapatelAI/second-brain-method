What exactly is the audit?

A 2–4 week operations audit of your mortgage shop. We interview you and each teammate (an hour each), review your systems and data read-only, and map how every loan actually moves — acquisition, delivery, support. You get the process maps, the count of where team hours actually go per file, and a ranked, data-backed fix list: what each fix does, where it lives in your stack, what it's worth. Plus quick wins your team can do that week.

What's the investment?

$6,000, one-time. If we continue into the build within 14 days of your audit presentation, the $6,000 is credited. Need a payment plan? No problem — reply to any of my emails or book a call and I'm happy to work with you.

How much of my time does it take?

About one hour from you, one hour from each teammate, and read-only access to your tools. That's it — this is completely done-for-you, end to end. We run the interviews, do the systems and data review, build the maps, and do the counting while your shop keeps running. If we continue into builds, those are done-for-you too.

How fast will I see results?

Honest answer: the audit itself is diagnosis, not magic — 2–4 weeks to map, count, and rank. Quick wins your team can act on usually land the week the audit is presented. The builds behind the bigger fixes come after, in ROI order — one meaningful build at a time, on your tools, with your team adopting as we go. Realistic arc: diagnosed in a month, first fixes live shortly after, compounding from there. Anyone promising an overnight transformation is selling you the bot that asks borrowers weird questions.

Do you actually know mortgages?

I don't do mortgages — I do operations and data. You're the mortgage expert; I map how the work moves and where it leaks, and I've been doing exactly that inside multiple mortgage shops this year. The problems the audit finds — leads lost before they're logged, document chasing, data entered three times, status that lives in one person's head — are operational, and they show up in every brokerage we've mapped. That's also why the audit interviews YOUR team instead of assuming: the map is built from how your shop actually works, not from a template.

How does the tech work? What do you actually build?

We build on what you have. Say you use Encompass and GoHighLevel — do your tools talk to each other? That handoff is usually the first build. If you're already set up on n8n or Zapier with other automations, we build using that. If you're already using Claude, we use that. If you don't have the tooling, we recommend the best tools for your use case — and only then. We have no interest in adding more tools to your stack; scatter is the problem, and we want to minimize it. In some cases we build out custom products, when that's what the operation actually needs. Every solution is rooted in your data — which is why the audit is the key that starts it all.

Will my team actually use it?

This is the smartest question we get, and it's the reason the audit exists. Every build comes out of your team's own interviews — their words, their workflow, their comfort level with the tools. We're not installing our system; we're removing friction from theirs. If we create something they want to use, you won't have to mandate it. And if a build wouldn't get used, it doesn't get built.

Can we skip the audit and go straight to building?

No — and it's the one thing I won't flex on. I did it once: the client was sure they knew the problem, I built what they asked, it worked, and it still didn't move their business — because the problem was misdiagnosed. I'd rather lose a deal than skip the map. Everything else about the engagement is customizable; the audit is the non-negotiable first step because it's what makes the build data-backed instead of guessed.

I'm not ready — still building my team / want my foundation first.

The map is the foundation. You don't pour concrete and then survey the lot. Hire before mapping and the new person inherits the leaks; map first and every hire lands in a system that shows them the work. "Not ready for the build" is often true — the build can wait. Not-ready-for-the-map is the expensive kind of patience: the leak runs while you wait.

Shouldn't I just hire a processor or an LOA?

Sometimes, yes — and the map will tell you which seat. But count first: a contract processor runs about $1,000 per file, forever, and a hire multiplies whatever system they land in. Clean system: multiplied capacity. Leaky system: multiplied leak. Nine times out of ten, "we need to hire" turns out to be "the work is wired wrong."

What if what I really need is more leads?

Then we're probably not it, and I'll tell you that on the call. We don't sell leads — we build capacity, so the leads you already pay for stop dying in inboxes and the team can serve more volume without breaking. If your operations are genuinely solid and lead flow is the only gap, you need marketing, not us.

Is there a guarantee?

We don't do a guarantee, on purpose. Anyone who guarantees specific results before mapping your operation is guessing with your money. What we commit to is deliverables: the maps, the hour count, and the ranked fix list are yours — and every build we then take on is scoped to a result, not to hours.

What happens after the audit?

You're holding the maps and the ranked fix list either way — build with us, build with your own people, or sit on it. If we build together, the $6,000 credits toward it and we work the list top-down, on your tools. No lock-in, no surprise retainer.
