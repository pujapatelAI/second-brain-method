discovery → proposal

day 3 was prepping the call. today is what happens after it: turning that call into a scoped proposal — same day, while the problem is still loud in their head. but more than the proposal itself, today is about making it a system — the same path every time, so it stops living in your head.

the system: discovery → proposal

a proposal you write from a blank doc every time isn't a system — it's a decision you keep re-making. a system is the same path every time. these questions turn "how i sometimes do it" into yours.

1
where do your call notes live — and do you record your meetings? the whole point of systems is getting the work out of your head, so your notes can't be scribbles. give the call your full attention and let an AI notetaker capture the transcript.your last 5 calls — how many do you have a transcript of? ___
2
what does your proposal need to answer for the client? the handful of questions someone needs answered before they'll commit to pay.for example: the problem in their words · the outcome they want · the scope of the work · the terms (price / timeline / next step). yours may differ — these are a starting point, not a rule.
3
do your last 3 proposals share a structure — or do you rebuild each one? this is where operations start: you can templatize. upload your last 3–10 proposals to claude and ask what structure they share.last 3 proposals — one skeleton, or 3 rebuilds? ___
+
bonus: how many hours from end of discovery call to proposal sent? this is where standards live — a target that protects quality every time. and the faster it goes out, the more likely you close.last proposal — call-end → sent = ___ hours · target: same day.

transcript tools: i use Otter; Granola and Fathom are great too — any works. comment on the video if you want a rec.

the system on one line

same path every time — that's what makes it a system instead of a scramble:

triggerthe discovery call ends. that's the start of the clock — not "when i get to it."
inputthe recording / transcript + your offer menu + a past proposal that closed.
processcapture the 4 answers → draft the scoped proposal.
outputone scoped proposal, the same structure every time — a template, not a blank page.
standardsent the same day, scoped to one problem, written in their words.

the 3 mistakes in the system

1 — you don't record the meeting. your memory only goes so far, and the whole point of good operations is getting everything out of your brain. don't reduce the call to a few scribbled lines — give it your full attention and let an AI notetaker capture the transcript. no transcript = nothing to turn into a proposal.

2 — there's no fixed output. you start from a blank document every time instead of templating what you've already been doing. that's exactly when a proposal comes out slow, bloated, and different every time.

3 — no discovery structure, so you ask the wrong questions (this is why day 3 comes first). the result: the proposal ends up being only what you'll do — your deliverables, hours, and money — instead of their problem, how you solve it, and what's in and out of scope. by the time they read it you're not in front of them, so the proposal has to re-run the whole sale on its own.

fix these three, establish the manual system first — then you can delegate it to AI. that's the next video.

hand the system to AI

this only works if you've actually built the system above — you need the pieces in place before you delegate. once they are, you connect it up and let claude run it:

·
connect your meeting-transcript app so it can read the calls
·
connect your calendar so it knows who you met and when
·
save your structure once — "make this the structured output for all future proposals, and remember it" → it lives as a .md file in your second brain

now it has your structure, your meetings, and your voice. so you just say:

"draft a proposal for [name]."

it finds them, pulls what you talked about, and drafts in your structure and voice — a proposal that took hours, down to about two minutes.

one step further — it doesn't wait for you to ask. connected to your calendar, it can spot a discovery call on its own, and about 30 minutes after the call ends — transcript ready — trigger itself and draft the proposal automatically. the call ending is the trigger; the system runs without you.

but skip the earlier steps and it breaks: no transcript → it doesn't know what you discussed; no saved structure → it comes out different every time; no past proposals → it won't sound like you. the AI is only as good as the system underneath it.
