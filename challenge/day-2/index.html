the five questions — put a real number on each

1
of your last 10 leads, how many ended up on a call?number: count them.
2
how many of those calls were never going to buy? wrong budget, wrong problem, wrong person in the room.number: that count × your call length = hours you donated.
3
your ICP in one sentence: [who] with [what problem] who can invest [range].can you write it without pausing? if not — this is today's real assignment. everything else derives from it.
4
your three must-clears, pulled from that sentence.three, not ten — a 10-point checklist kills intake (yesterday's lesson cuts both ways).
5
what happens to a "not this offer" lead right now? routed somewhere — or silence?silence is lost revenue AND lost referrals.

what qualification actually checks

the criteria aren't invented — they fall out of your ICP sentence. the usual suspects:

budget / revenue level · industry or niche · the problem they name (is it one you've solved before?) · timeline & urgency · who actually decides (authority = can say yes to money — a manager who "has to run it up" doesn't clear) · team size or stage · the red flags you've learned the hard way ("quick call to pick your brain," discount-openers, scope-creepers).

pick the three that protect your calendar most. write them down. that's your bar — and once it's written, the rule takes the heat instead of you.

the triage tree — routing, not rejecting

triggera logged lead with the basics (yesterday's output)
processscore against your three must-clears
outputone of four doors:
1
clears all three → the call / your main offer. booking link, no deliberation. this is the whole point — yes should be automatic.
2
right person, smaller budget → your lower offer. a group program, a smaller package, a digital product. not a rejection — a different door.
3
right budget, wrong problem → a referral out. with a real reason. the goodwill outlives the lead.
4
can't score it → one clarifying question first. unknown is not failed — plenty of great leads don't lead with numbers. ask, never guess-score.

mine, as a live example: needs the full operations build → we get on a call. wants the system, smaller budget → my monthly program. just curious how this works → this challenge. same intake, three doors.

the one nuance that saves good leads

a lead that misses exactly one criterion is the interesting pile — the perfect-fit problem with a small budget, the referral from a past client, the dream industry that can't pay full rate yet. those deserve a two-minute human look, not an auto-decline. a lead that misses two or more can be routed mechanically.

and when the answer is a true no, use a template so it never gets skipped out of awkwardness:

"thanks for reaching out. based on what you've described, I don't think we're the right fit — [one specific honest reason]. if that changes, or if it'd help, happy to point you toward someone who works in that range."

name the actual reason. vague declines generate a "why?" reply — which puts the interruption right back on your desk.

the three mistakes almost everyone makes

1 — qualifying on the call. paying 45 minutes to learn what one intake question would have told you. yesterday's system collects the basics; today's rules read them before the calendar opens.

2 — no written ICP, so the bar lives in your gut. you re-decide every lead from scratch, which means the answer depends on your week and how empty your calendar looks. write the sentence once. let the rule take the heat.

3 — treating triage as yes/no. a "not a fit for this offer" lead is often a customer for a different one. if every almost-fit gets declined — or worse, silence — you're filtering out your own revenue.

how to hand it to claude

give claude three things: your ICP sentence, your three must-clears, and your last 10 leads with how each turned out. have it draft your triage tree and a response for each door.

then the daily workflow: paste a new lead's intake row → claude scores it, names the door, and drafts the reply. you approve and send.

the guardrails stay yours: unknowns get the clarifying question, never a guessed score · a lead that misses exactly one criterion always lands on your desk · referrals from past clients always reach you, regardless of score.
