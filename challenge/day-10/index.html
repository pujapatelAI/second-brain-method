first — a plan is not a to-do list

three things get muddled — each does a different job:

briefsays what you're building + who + what "done" means. (day 9)
plansays when + in what order — the schedule you run against. (today)
to-do listtasks with no order and no dates — not a plan. it keeps you busy; it can't tell you if you'll make the deadline.

your milestones already live in the brief. today you take each one and break it down to dated, sorted tasks — so you can tell, today, whether you'll hit it.

the system: milestones down to dated tasks

take each milestone, break it into tasks, sort them, and date them. put a real number where you can — the gaps show themselves.

1
what are your milestone deadlines? break the project into milestones and put a deadline on each. if you did day 9, they're already sitting in your kickoff brief.milestones written, each with a date: ___
2
what are the literal tasks — with verbs — to hit each milestone? say the milestone is launch ads: write the scripts, client films them, edit the videos. and of those, which can run at the same time vs which have to go in order? (write the scripts + set up the account together; you can't edit until the videos exist; you can't film until the scripts are done.)tasks for your next milestone: ___ · how many can run in parallel? ___
3
which of those tasks need client input? the ones where the client has to do something or hand something over — that's where the timeline leaves your hands, so you flag it now.tasks waiting on the client: ___
4
by when does each piece have to land — with buffer? back-time from the client's deadline and leave yourself room, because something always comes up. (they expect it Nov 17 → your plan lands it before then.)buffer days between your finish and their deadline: ___

the system on one line

take the brief's milestones and turn them into a timeline:

triggera project's starting — you've got the brief.
inputthe milestones + deadlines already in your kickoff brief.
processbreak each milestone into tactical tasks → sort them side-by-side vs in-order → flag what needs the client → back-time every piece with buffer.
outputa timeline you can look at today and know you'll hit — buffer and all.
standardevery project runs off a plan built this way; you're never guessing whether you're on track.

the 3 mistakes in the system

1 — a to-do list is not a plan. a list of everything you have to do isn't a timeline — no finish line, no order, just a pile. you stay busy, but you can't tell if you're on track until the deadline is right on top of you.

2 — you line everything up back-to-back. you treat every task like it has to wait for the one before it, so the project drags on way longer than it needs to — when half of it could've run at the same time. (or the flip: you assume it can all happen at once, and the one thing you needed first holds up everything behind it.) a real plan knows which is which.

3 — you plan like you're the only one in it. your timeline only has your tasks on it — nothing for what you need from the client and by when. so the first time they're slow, the whole thing slips, and from the outside it looks like you missed the deadline.

fix these three — give it a finish line and an order, sort what runs side-by-side vs what waits, and put the client's deadlines on the plan too — and a slip shows up while there's still time to catch it.
