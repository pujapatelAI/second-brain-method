lead intake & capture

how your leads come in, where they go, and what happens next. today you write it down — that's the whole assignment. most people have never looked at this end to end, and it shows up later as lost leads, slow replies, and seven tabs of chaos.

the five questions — put a real number on each

1
where are your leads coming from? every source — organic, meta ads, referrals, dms.number: how many from each, last 30 days?
2
where does each one go? your crm? a link-in-bio form? do they just stay inside meta? your dms?number: count the destinations. more than one is worth noticing.
3
do you get notified when a lead lands? yes or no — per source.a source with no notification is a source that leaks.
4
how long until you reach out or reply?number: hours (or days — be honest) for your last five leads.
5
do you get the info you need at intake? not qualifying — the basics: name, business, what they're asking for.number: of your last five leads, how many did you have to chase for basics?

now write it as a process

what you just described has a shape. write yours exactly like this:

triggera new lead lands — from any source
processit goes somewhere → you get notified → you reach out → you collect the basics
outputa logged lead, with the info you need, that's heard back from you

that's it. you just documented your lead intake & capture process — the first system out of your head. everything we build this month starts from pieces written exactly like this.

find the bottleneck

now look at your numbers from the five questions and mark three letters on your process:

B
bottleneck — where more comes in than gets through. ten leads in, three replied to: the other seven are sitting right there.
H
handoff — every time the same info moves between tools or people (meta → spreadsheet → your dms). handoffs are where leads get dropped.
D
dependency — a step that can't happen until another one does. you can't reply fast if you never got notified.

the step with the worst number is where your fix starts. keep what you marked — thursday's q&a exists for exactly this.

the two mistakes almost everyone makes

1 — five sources, five destinations. tiktok leads in your dms, meta leads inside meta, referrals in your texts. there's no one place where all your leads exist — so you check seven tools just to know what's going on. the fix is one source of truth: pick the destination that's most flexible with the sources you already have — usually a crm — and funnel everything into it, tagged by source. even referrals: send them a short form that lands in the same place.

2 — intake with no information. a lead hits your list with no name, no business, no ask — that's not intake, that's a notification. you don't need revenue level or icp fit yet (that's qualifying — tomorrow's system). you need the one layer of basics that keeps your time from being wasted.

picking a crm (if you don't have one)

two inputs decide it: which tools your leads already come from (integrations), and your own comfort with tech. six that come up constantly:

crmbest for
HubSpot (free)the default starting point — free, clean, connects to almost everything. unsure? start here.
GoHighLevelmarketing-led businesses running ads + funnels + sms. the heaviest all-in-one; steepest learning curve.
HoneyBooksolo service pros — consultants, coaches, creatives — who want intake forms, proposals, and invoices in one place.
Dubsadoclient-workflow-heavy services (photographers, designers). deep automation, more setup up front.
Pipedriveyou mainly need a simple, visual pipeline of deals. easiest to learn.
Airtablethe build-it-yourself option if you're comfortable with tech. total flexibility; pairs with zapier/make.

the crm is the tool — the system is the flow you wrote above; a crm just holds it. still stuck on the pick? bring your setup to thursday's q&a.
