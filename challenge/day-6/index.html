The 30-Day Brain Extract · Day 6 · Phase 1 — Getting Clients

follow-up & reactivation

day 5 was the close — the yes or the no. but most leads don't hand you either. they just go quiet: the proposal nobody answered, the warm lead you meant to circle back to, the client you haven't talked to in months. the money was already in the room — it just went silent. today's system makes sure quiet never turns into gone, and pulls dormant clients back into paying ones. and like everything else, the win is making it a system — so no warm lead depends on you happening to remember.

the system: follow-up & reactivation

"i'll circle back" isn't a system — it's a promise to your memory, and your memory is busy. a system is the same thing happening every time a lead goes quiet, whether or not you remember it. these questions turn "i follow up when i think of it" into yours — put a real number on each, and the leak shows itself.

1
who's gone quiet right now — and how many? count the warm leads and open proposals sitting with no next step. that number is the leak. this is your trigger: silence past a certain point should set something off, not just sit there.warm leads + open proposals with no scheduled next touch: ___
2
what's your rule for when a follow-up fires? how many days of quiet before you reach out — and how many touches before you stop. no rule means it only happens when you happen to remember, which is the whole problem. (most yeses land on touch 3–5, not touch 1.)your line: follow up after ___ days · up to ___ touches
3
where does each lead's status live? one place you can see who's waiting on you vs. who you're waiting on. if it's scattered across your inbox, texts, and head, nothing can surface the ones going cold.how many separate places a lead's status might live right now? ___
4
what do you actually say? a message ready per situation — quiet warm lead, proposal-sent-no-reply, past-client reactivation — so you're not rewriting "just checking in" from a blank page every time.of your 3 core situations, how many have a message written? ___ / 3
+
reactivation: which past clients haven't heard from you in 90+ days? the warmest leads you have are the people who already paid you. a reach-out on a schedule turns "one-and-done" into repeat and referral.past clients with no contact in 90+ days: ___

the system on one line

same path every time — that's what turns "i meant to" into a system:

triggera lead or client crosses your silence line — X days quiet, a proposal unanswered, or a past client hits 90 days.
inputyour one lead/client list with last-contact dates + your message-per-situation bank.
processsurface who's overdue → pick the situation → send the right message → log the next touch date.
outputevery warm lead has a scheduled next touch, and every dormant client gets a reach-out. nothing dies in silence.
standardno lead stays quiet past your line without a follow-up. ever.

the 3 mistakes in the system

1 — follow-up lives in your memory. you "mean to" circle back, but there's no trigger — so it only happens when you happen to remember. and the ones you forget are usually the ones that had the money. the fix isn't discipline, it's a trigger: silence past your line sets something off on its own.

2 — you stop after one. one "just checking in," no reply, and you quietly give up because a second feels pushy. but most yeses come on touch 3–5 — quitting after one leaves the deal on the table for the person who followed up a third time. the cadence is the system; decide it once so awkwardness doesn't get a vote.

3 — every follow-up starts from a blank page. you rewrite "just following up" from scratch each time, so it's slow, it feels like a chore, and it shows. a message per situation — written once — means you follow up in seconds and it reads as intentional, not needy.

fix these three — a trigger, a cadence, and a message bank — and follow-up stops being the thing you feel guilty about and becomes the thing quietly closing your deals.

build your follow-up message bank

the fix for mistake #3 — and the asset that makes every future follow-up take seconds. write your version of each once, in your voice, and never start from blank again. three to start with:

1 · the quiet warm lead hey [name] — no pressure at all, just didn't want [the thing they wanted] to fall off your plate. still worth a conversation, or is the timing off right now? either answer is totally fine.
2 · proposal sent, no reply hi [name] — following up on the proposal i sent [when]. happy to hop on a quick call to walk through it or adjust anything. what's the best way to help you get to a yes or no?
3 · past-client reactivation (90+ days) hey [name] — you've been on my mind. we wrapped [project] a while back and i'd love to hear how [the outcome] is holding up. i've also got [new thing] that might fit — want me to send details?

write each in your words, save it, reuse it. that's the bank — your follow-up, out of your head and onto the page.

do it with Claude — two speeds

this is the video-3 payoff, and the whole point of building the system first: once the trigger, the cadence, and the message bank exist, Claude can run the follow-up for you — at whichever speed you're ready for.

speed one — you're still driving. you ask Claude "who's gone quiet on me?" it reads your lead list, hands you the overdue pile (this proposal's silent 4 days, this lead 9, this client 3 months), and drafts each follow-up for its exact situation in your voice. you skim, approve, send. it did the remembering and the writing — thirty seconds instead of an afternoon.

speed two — you don't even see it. once you trust it, the rule just runs: anything quiet past your line gets a follow-up drafted and queued, reactivation notes go out to clients hitting 90 days, and the next-touch date logs itself. you find out it worked when someone replies "good to hear from you."

Claude never invents your follow-up — it runs the one you built. the system is what makes the automation safe. build it first (the questions above), hand it over second.
