The 30-Day Brain Extract · Day 5 · Phase 1 — Getting Clients

proposal → close

day 4 got the proposal out the door. today is the biggest-money moment in the whole pipeline: turning that sent proposal into a signed client — the close. and like everything else, the goal is to make it a system — the same path from "sent" to "decided," so your best deals stop depending on your mood that day.

the system: proposal → close

sending a proposal and hoping isn't a system — it's a coin flip you re-run every deal. a system is the same path from "sent" to "decided," every time. these questions turn "how i sometimes do it" into yours.

1
do you present the proposal, or just send it? the close starts at delivery — walking them through it (live, or a short recorded loom) keeps you in the room for the decision. emailing a pdf and waiting hands the decision to silence.your last 5 proposals — how many did you walk through vs just send? ___
2
what's the one next step you set every time a proposal goes out? a system has a defined next move — a booked decision date, not "let me know your thoughts." open-ended is exactly where deals go quiet (and become day 6's follow-up problem).your last few proposals — how many had a real decision date vs an open "let me know"? ___
3
what objections do you hear over and over — and are your answers written down? price, timing, "i need to think about it," "i need to check with my partner." if you improvise the response each time, the close lives in your head. a system keeps a bank of your best answers, ready to pull.your top 3 objections — how many have a written response ready? ___ / 3
+
bonus: what happens the second they say yes? is the contract + deposit ready to fire, or does momentum leak while you scramble to "send something over"? the fastest path from yes → signed is part of the close.last "yes" — how many hours from yes to signed / deposit? ___

the system on one line

same path every time — that's what makes it a system instead of a scramble:

triggerthe proposal is delivered. the clock to a decision starts now.
inputthe proposal + your offer / pricing + your objection bank.
processpresent it → set a decision date → handle objections → ask for the yes.
outputa real decision — yes, no, or a booked decision — never an open "let me know."
standardevery proposal gets a real ask and a dated next step. no deal dies in silence.

the 3 mistakes in the system

1 — you don't present it, you just send it. you email the pdf and wait — but now the proposal has to sell itself, and you're not in the room to answer anything. walk them through it (live, or a two-minute recorded video) and you stay part of the decision.

2 — you never actually ask for the decision. you end with "let me know your thoughts." it feels polite, but it's open-ended — and open-ended is where deals go quiet. no decision date, no next step, so it drifts. that's a follow-up problem, not a close.

3 — you improvise your objections. you hear the same three or four every time — too expensive, wrong timing, "let me think about it," "i need to check with my partner." if you're making up the answer in the moment, you're winging the most important part of the sale. those answers should already be written down.

fix these three — present it, ask for the decision, and have your objections handled — and you've got an actual close system.

build your objection bank

the fix for mistake #3 — and the one asset that makes every future close easier. list the objections you hear over and over, and write your best response to each, once. then you never improvise the highest-stakes moment again.

the usual four to start with — add your own:

·
"it's too expensive"
·
"it's not the right time"
·
"let me think about it"
·
"i need to check with my partner"

write your response to each in your words, save it, reuse it. that's the bank — the close, finally out of your head and onto the page.

no "do it with AI" today — on purpose

every other day ends with a video on handing the system to AI. not this one. the close is a human moment — presenting, reading the room, asking for the decision, handling a real objection live. AI drafted your proposal on day 4; it can't close it for you. this one's yours.
