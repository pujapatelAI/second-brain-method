The 30-Day Brain Extract · Day 13 · Phase 2 — Delivering the Work

meeting prep & agendas

yesterday's weekly note keeps a client from having to ask where things stand. but you still get on calls — and those are the most expensive thing on your calendar, the only thing that costs everyone their time at the same moment. today is the system that makes the meetings you do have worth the time: the agenda, built before anyone joins.

if the meeting is just a readout, it was an email

here's the trap that turns a 30-minute call into an hour with nothing decided: you get on to "check in" or "sync." but a meeting with no decision to make can't end — it just runs out of time, and you book another one to finish.

a meeting is the most expensive thing on your calendar — it's the only thing that spends everyone's time at once. so it has to earn it. the agenda is what earns it. if the only thing that's going to happen is you telling them where things stand, that was yesterday's status update — not a meeting.

which gives you the one rule this whole system runs on: every agenda item is a decision or a question — never a topic. "budget" is a topic; it wanders until someone gets tired. "do we add the two grand or cut the scope" is a decision; it ends. write the line as the thing that has to be settled, and the meeting settles it.

the system: three questions

the whole agenda comes out of three questions. answer them before you send the invite and the meeting runs itself. write your answers in.

1
what does this meeting need to produce — what has to be true at the end that isn't true now? a decision made, an alignment reached, a blocker cleared. if you can't name it, you don't need the meeting — you need the note from day 12.your next scheduled call — name its one outcome in a sentence: ___
2
what do you need to walk in already knowing — and where does it come from? not memory the morning of. three sources: what's moved since last time, what's sitting on their side, and the big one — last meeting's action items, with a check on which actually got done.where do those facts live right now — the tracker, or your head? ___
3
what do they need before they show up — and does it go out ahead of time? the agenda isn't your private notes. it's sent before, so the client arrives knowing what you need decided and brings whatever they need to bring. a cold-open meeting is where "let me get back to you" comes from.do you send an agenda before your calls now? ___ (if no — that's the whole fix)

the system on one line

turn a slot on the calendar into a meeting that ends with decisions made:

triggerthere's a meeting on the calendar.
inputthe tracker, the open threads, and last meeting's action items — where the work already lives.
processname the one outcome → pull what's moved / what's on their side / what got done → turn each into a decision or a question, not a topic → send it before so they come ready.
outputa meeting that starts in motion, ends with decisions made and next steps owned — and never needs a second meeting to redo the first.
standardthe agenda goes out before every call. no agenda, no meeting — it becomes the day-12 note instead.

what it looks like finished

the agenda for a check-in on the same project you planned on day 10, assigned on day 11, and reported on day 12 — Riverside Lending, paid-social launch, due nov 17. notice every line is a decision or a question, and it turns last week's "waiting on you" into something that gets settled live:

Riverside — project check-in
Thu Oct 9 · 30 min · sent the day before
goal leave this call with the videos approved and the ad-account access unblocked, so the build can start monday.
decision the 6 edited videos are back from review — approve as-is, or flag changes today. editing's done; this is the gate.
decision ad-account access has been pending on your side for 2 weeks — can we grant it live on this call?
question still tracking nov 17 — anything on your end we should plan the timeline around?
before we hang up confirm who's sending the access and by when.

they walk in already knowing the two calls they have to make — so the 30 minutes gets spent deciding, not discovering. that's the whole output of this system.

the 3 mistakes in the system

1 — the meeting has no outcome; you're just "checking in." if the only thing that's going to happen is everyone saying where things stand, that was an email — that was day 12. a meeting with no decision to make can't end, it just runs out of time. before you send the invite, name the one thing that has to be true when it's over. if you can't, cancel it.

2 — your agenda is a list of topics, not decisions. "discuss timeline, review the designs, talk budget." topics have no finish line, so you circle them until someone runs out of patience. every line should be the actual decision or question — "approve the homepage direction or send it back," "push the date or cut a feature." a topic wanders; a decision ends.

3 — you build it live instead of sending it ahead. the agenda lives in your head, so the client shows up cold, you spring decisions on them they haven't thought about, and they say the thing that costs you a whole extra week — "let me get back to you." send the agenda before, with what you need from them, so they walk in ready to decide.

fix these three and the meeting does its job — one call, decisions made, everybody clear. no "quick follow-up" to redo the thing you already met about.

do it with Claude — build the routine once

the tactical version, and the whole point of the challenge. don't re-explain your agenda format and where the facts live every time you've got a call — do that and you re-teach it before every meeting. you teach it once, then it drafts the agenda the moment a meeting lands on your calendar.

1
let it interview you. tell Claude: "interview me about how i prep for a client meeting, then save it as a skill i can run before any call." it walks you through the three questions above — what a meeting has to produce, where you pull the facts from, what the client needs before they join, what your agenda looks like. you answer once.
2
it writes the skill. Claude turns your answers into a saved routine with the rules baked in — "every line is a decision or a question, never a topic," "always pull last meeting's action items and check what got done," "the agenda goes out the day before." that saved skill is the extract. you never explain your prep again.
3
set it to run on the trigger. now, ten minutes before a call — or the moment the meeting hits your calendar — it reads the tracker, your threads, and your meeting notes, and hands you a draft: the one outcome, each item as a decision or question, and the short note to send the client first. you skim, fix a line, approve.

you're not asking Claude to be clever before every meeting — you taught it your prep once, and now the routine builds the agenda for you. the process came out of your head and into something that runs on its own — which is the entire challenge, in one system.
