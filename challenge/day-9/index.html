The 30-Day Brain Extract · Day 9 · Phase 2 — Delivering the Work

the kickoff brief

day 8 got everyone set up. today — before you build a single thing — you write the one doc that says exactly what you're delivering: the kickoff brief. it's not the proposal (that sold the deal), and it's not the contract (that's legal cover) — it's the working source of truth you, your team, and your AI actually build from. and you don't fill it top to bottom. you work backwards from the goal.

first — what a brief is not

three docs get confused all the time. each has a different job:

proposalwritten to win the deal — the sales doc, before yes. (day 4)
contractwritten to protect both of you — the legal terms, at yes. (day 7)
briefwritten to build it — your working spec, after onboarding. (today)

and a brief points at whoever needs to be aligned:

·
internal — aligns your team on the work (tasks, who does what). you need it the moment you're more than just you.
·
external — aligns the client on the deliverables (what they get, when).
·
often both — same brief, one copy pointed each way.

and it's not the project plan (that's tomorrow): the brief aligns everyone at a high level — the plan is the detailed schedule you execute against.

the system: work backwards from the goal

you don't write a brief top to bottom — you start at the goal and let it decide everything else. answer these five and the brief writes itself. put a real number where you can; the gaps show themselves.

1
who needs to be on the same page before you start — just you, your team, or the client too? that decides which brief you need: a team → an internal brief (align on the work); a client → an external one (agree on the deliverables); solo + simple → a few lines in your notes is enough.people building this besides you: ___ → 0 = keep it light · 1+ = write it down
2
what's the one goal everyone's aligning on — in their words? the real outcome they're paying for, concrete enough that you'll both know when it's hit. this is the anchor; everything below works backwards from it.can you say the goal in one sentence they'd nod at? write it: ___
3
what's in scope to reach it — and what's explicitly OUT? internally that's the work; externally it's the deliverables. the "out" list is your scope-creep wall — the line you point to when they ask for "one quick extra."things written under "out of scope": ___ (if it's 0, you have no wall)
4
who owns what — and who makes the call? everyone's lane, plus the one person who decides when there's a fork. this is what keeps decisions from stalling mid-project.name the decision-maker for this project: ___
5
how will you all stay synced — the checkpoints, and how you'll share progress? agree the rhythm now (how often, in what form) so alignment holds past day one. the full schedule is tomorrow; this is just the heartbeat.you'll send an update every ___ , via ___

the system on one line

start at the goal, and let it build the rest:

triggerthey're onboarded — before any real work starts.
inputthe signed proposal + what you learned onboarding + the goal in their words.
processwho needs aligning? (team / client / both) → set the goal → derive scope (in + out) → name roles + the decider → set the sync rhythm.
outputone brief everyone builds from — no drift, no scope creep, no "wait, what were we doing?"
standardevery project starts from a brief, sized to who's involved — a few lines solo, a shared doc with a team.

the 3 mistakes in the system

1 — you assume the proposal and contract have it covered. they signed, so you've got a proposal and a contract — and you just start building. but the proposal was written to sell, before you'd even seen inside; the contract is legal cover. neither one tells you what to actually build. start from those and you're building off a sales pitch.

2 — the goal is vague, or it's your words, not theirs. "build them a lead system" isn't a goal — it's a task. if the outcome isn't written the way they'd say it out loud, you've aimed at the wrong target. and since the whole brief works backwards from the goal, a fuzzy goal throws off everything under it.

3 — you name what's IN, but never what's OUT. you list every deliverable and stop there. so when they ask for "one quick extra," there's no wall to point to. that one "here's what's not included" line is what saves you from months of unpaid scope creep.

fix these three — build from a real brief, nail the goal in their words, and draw the out-of-scope line — and the project stays pointed at what they actually paid for, start to finish.

steal the template

one page, built backwards from the goal. keep it as light as the project needs — a few lines when it's just you, a shared doc when there's a team or a client to align.

the kickoff brief goal: [the outcome, in their words — concrete enough you'll both know it's done]
in scope: [what you're delivering]  ·  out of scope: [what you're not]
milestones: [the key dates at a glance — full schedule comes tomorrow]
roles: [who owns what · who makes the call]
sync: [how often you'll update · where]

internal or external? same skeleton — point it at your team (lean on roles + work) or at the client (lean on deliverables + milestones).
