the system: contract → kickoff handoff

closing the sale isn't the finish line — it's a handoff. a system is the same path from signed to started, every time, so it never depends on you remembering the steps at 9pm. these questions turn "however i onboarded the last one" into yours — put a real number on each, and the gaps show themselves.

1
the second they say yes — what locks it in, and how fast? contract signed and deposit paid, right then, while certainty is at its peak. a lag here is exactly where cold feet and "let me check with my partner" creep back in.last "yes" — hours from yes to signed + deposit collected? ___
2
what do you need FROM them to start — one intake, or a chase? access, logins, assets, the answers only they have. gathered in one kickoff intake, or dragged out of them over a week of one-off emails?to fully onboard your last client, how many separate asks did it take? ___
3

the system on one line

same path every time — that's what turns a scramble into a kickoff:

triggerthe client says yes — signs and/or pays the deposit.
inputyour contract + deposit link, your kickoff intake, your welcome template, your project template.
processlock it in (contract + deposit) → send the welcome (what's next + timeline) → send the one intake → book the kickoff call → set up the project.
outputsigned becomes started — client onboarded, everything gathered, momentum intact, nothing dropped.
standardevery new client goes from yes to fully kicked off the same way — ideally the same day.

the 3 mistakes in the system

1 — the gap between "yes" and "signed." you don't have the contract and deposit ready to fire the second they say yes, so there's a lag — you go find the template, customize it, send it that evening. but certainty is highest at yes and it decays by the hour. that gap is where "let me check with my partner" and cold feet live. the fix isn't being faster — it's having it ready to send in the moment.

2 — death by a thousand asks. you collect what you need to start piecemeal — logins today, brand assets next week, that one answer whenever you remember. so the project stalls in intake limbo and you look disorganized before you've done any real work. one kickoff intake that gathers everything at once fixes it.

3 — the post-yes silence. right after the most excited moment in the whole relationship, the client hears… nothing, while you "get set up behind the scenes." silence right after yes is exactly where buyer's remorse grows. the handoff has to include an immediate welcome — what's next, the timeline, how to reach you — not leave a vacuum.

fix these three — lock it in the moment they say yes, gather everything in one intake, welcome them right away — and the handoff stops being a scramble and becomes the reason they're glad they picked you.

build your kickoff kit

two assets that make every future kickoff run itself. first, the one intake that collects everything you need to start — so you never chase it piecemeal again. the four buckets:

·
access & logins — accounts, tools, platforms you'll need in
·
assets & brand — files, logos, docs, examples, anything they already have
·
the answers only they have — goals, deadlines, decision-makers, must-knows
·
points of contact — who you talk to, for what, and how

then the welcome — sent the moment they sign, so the excitement never hits silence:

the kickoff welcome welcome aboard, [name] — genuinely glad we're doing this. here's exactly what happens next: [step 1] by [date], then [step 2]. your kickoff call is [when] — [link]. i've also sent a quick intake to grab what i need to hit the ground running; fill it whenever you get a sec. anything at all, just reply here — you've got me directly.

build these two once — the intake and the welcome — and the handoff runs on templates, not memory.

do it with Claude — two speeds

this is the video-3 payoff: once the kit exists (the intake, the welcome, the project template), Claude can run the whole handoff for you — at whichever speed you're ready for.

speed one — you're still driving. you tell Claude "[client] just signed — kick them off." from that one line it writes the welcome in your voice, builds the intake, drops the kickoff call link, and sets up the project. you skim, approve, send. signed to fully onboarded in under a minute.

speed two — you don't even see it. once you trust it, the moment a deal gets marked closed the whole kickoff fires on its own — contract confirmed, welcome out, intake sent, call booked, project created. you find out it worked when the client replies "this is the most organized onboarding i've ever seen."

Claude runs the kickoff you built — it doesn't invent it. the kit is what makes the automation safe. build it first (the questions above), hand it over second.
