approval & sign-off

the revised version (day 17) went back. now you need the actual yes — and this is where a "looks good" in a text becomes "i never approved that." sign-off isn't chasing a decision. it's making the yes a real event: dated, on a specific version, from the person allowed to give it — so "approved" means locked, and the work can close.

"approved" is an event you create, not a reply you wait for

a deliverable stays open for weeks not because the client is slow — but because no one asked for a real yes. it goes back, "seems fine," everyone moves on, nothing locks. the fix: make sign-off a deliberate step, not a vibe that drifts by.

a real sign-off is three things: asked for (not assumed from silence), from the person allowed to give it (not whoever's in the thread), and written down (approved, which version, who, when). miss one and "approved" won't hold.

the yes has to come from the decider, not a stand-in. "looks great" from the day-to-day contact feels like progress — until the person who owns the budget sees it and reopens the whole thing.

and the yes has to change something. once signed off, the work locks: next phase starts, invoice goes, anything new becomes new scope. a sign-off that locks nothing is just another comment.

the system: four questions

answer these about how work gets approved in your business, and sign-off stops being a soft "looks good" and becomes the step that closes things.

1
the gate — does finished work clear a sign-off, or ship on whoever did it?
2
the right yes — is it clear who's allowed to approve each kind of work, or does it default to you?
3
the record — is the yes written down, or a verbal "go ahead" nobody can find later?
4
the lock — once signed off, is it final, or does it keep reopening after the yes?

the 3 mistakes in the system

1 — nothing actually has to be approved. work ships because someone finished it, not because the right person said go — so things go out that shouldn't have, and no one owns the yes.

2 — the yes is verbal and disappears. "go ahead" on a call or a text, no what · who · when. weeks later nobody can point to it, and "i never approved that" wins.

3 — signed off doesn't mean locked. it gets approved, then keeps getting reopened and re-touched — so the yes settles nothing and the next thing never starts.

fix these three and "approved" becomes a line you can point to — dated, owned, final — not a feeling everyone half-remembers.

do it with Claude — on my real delivery process

a "done" deliverable across twelve clients, a group program, a software, and live workshops stays done because i don't rely on remembering who approved what — i run the checkpoint through my company brain in Claude Code. same premise as every day: you build the rules first — what needs a sign-off, who can give it, what "approved" records — so Claude runs the gate, doesn't decide it.

1
it packages finished work into one approval request — what changed, which version — routed to the person who signs off, not the thread.
2
when the yes comes back, it records it — approved · who · when · version — tied to that deliverable, so "did we approve this?" is a one-second lookup.
3
anything after the yes, it flags as new work against the locked version — so "approved" holds instead of quietly reopening.

the line you keep both hands on: Claude asks for the yes, routes it, records it, guards the boundary — i decide the work's ready, and whether that "approved" is a real yes or a soft "i guess." Claude never gives the yes; it builds the gate around it.
