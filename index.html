A

Multi-location ophthalmology practice

3 locations · 300+ referring physicians · three-month build, 2026

The problem

A full-time physician liaison visited referring doctors in the field, and nothing connected that investment to a result. Referral counts lived in Excel, visit logs in a route app, patient attribution in the EMR, and the call center emailed a screenshot of a pivot table. No system could say which doctors were actually producing or where the liaison should go next week.

What we built

One resolved identity per referring doctor across all four sources, then four views on top of it: referral activity, liaison performance, physician profiles and where to go next. Three user roles, with patient data visible only to the administrator. Built HIPAA-aligned from the start, on cloud infrastructure in the practice's own name, through six milestone gates with a written definition of done.

What changed

  • Live on HTTPS with multi-factor authentication, on infrastructure the practice owns
  • All four pipelines running on the practice's real data
  • Visits and referrals can finally be read against each other
  • Expanded by change order into two more modules

D

150-person multi-location retail company

150 employees · 11 departments · six-month engagement, 2025 to 2026

The problem

No central operating system. Over 1,200 tasks across 11 departments tracked in spreadsheets, email threads and verbal handoffs. The CEO was the single point of coordination for field operations, inventory, HR, training and repairs, and the only way to know what was on track was to chase every department head personally.

What we built

A structural audit mapping the whole organization into 11 departments, 39 pillars, 94 categories and 234 subcategories. Then every recurring task captured with six fields (trigger, action, completion condition, dependencies, hours a month, position) and reviewed line by line in two to three rounds, with each department director. Tasks became workflows, with the handoffs between roles and the dependencies between tasks written down.

What changed

  • The CEO went from zero cross-department visibility to a view of all 11 departments
  • The weekly manual coordination cycle that consumed leadership time is gone
  • Redundant processes found across 3 departments; data gaps behind recurring issues surfaced
  • Both contracted deliverables signed off by the company's directors

Questions

What owners ask before the call.

What happens on the call?
We check fit, I walk you through how the audit and the build work, and we decide. I don't map or audit your business on the call. That is the first paid phase, and it takes more than 30 minutes to do properly.
Do you build it, or just tell us what to do?
Both. The audit ends in a roadmap you could hand to your own team, and some clients do. Most have me and my builders do the building, in milestones, with your sign-off at each one. Nothing ships without it.
How much of my team's time does the audit take?
Hours, not weeks. One kickoff conversation, an intake questionnaire, interviews with the people doing the work, voice memos when something occurs to them, and read-only access to the systems you already use so I see real volumes instead of guesses.
Is our data safe?
Everything is built inside approved, enterprise-grade, data-isolated tools where your data is not used to train anyone's model. Governance is the second layer we build, not an afterthought. Where patient or client data is involved, the architecture is built to that compliance floor from the start, the way the ophthalmology platform above was.
What does it cost?
The audit is a paid, fixed-scope engagement. The build is priced from what the audit finds and paid in milestones, so there is no price on this page. We go through how pricing works on the call.
Do you do marketing or lead generation?
No. This is operations, data and AI: delivering and converting what you already sell, with the team you already have.
We already tried AI and it didn't stick. Why would this be different?
Because what you tried was the top two layers, agents and apps, with nothing underneath them. The team went back to doing it by hand because the data, the context and the standards were still in people's heads. We build those first, in order.
